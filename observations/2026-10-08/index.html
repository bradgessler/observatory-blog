Night of 8 October 2026

Five objects from a crooked tripod and a clear, moonless night: the core of Andromeda, the Ring Nebula, the Crystal Ball, the Triangulum Galaxy and the Little Dumbbell.

  • The core of the Andromeda Galaxy Messier 31
  • The Ring Nebula Messier 57, a planetary nebula in Lyra
  • The Crystal Ball Nebula NGC 1514, a planetary nebula in Taurus
  • The Triangulum Galaxy Messier 33, a spiral galaxy in Triangulum
  • The Little Dumbbell Nebula Messier 76, a planetary nebula in Perseus

I set the tripod down in the driveway without leveling it, pushed one button, and the telescope worked out where it was pointing from four photos. After a break to show some kids things through the eyepiece, I put the camera back on and Claude had the telescope work through a list of targets on its own while I packed things up.

No darks this time, and no flats from dusk or dawn, so every picture was stacked with what the night's own frames could tell us. Two of the galaxies use a flat built from the open sky around the other targets. A smeared frame or a frame through cloud was dropped, and each picture's page says how many were kept.

Location
San Francisco Bay Area. A driveway at home, with trees on most sides
Sky
Clear and moonless, with one patch of thin cloud that crossed the Ring Nebula for eight minutes. Steady air late in the night
Telescope
Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain. 203 mm aperture, 2,082 mm focal length by plate solve, f/10.3
Camera
Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus. APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
Field
38.8 x 25.9 arcmin in one frame, 0.388 arcsec per pixel
Mount
Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, set down without leveling or polar alignment, 13.4 degrees off the pole. Aligned from four plate-solved photos and driven from that model
Computer
Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir
Frames
15 second exposures at ISO 3200, 24 to 33 taken per object and 14 to 30 kept
Processing
Stacked from the RAW frames with numpy, OpenCV and rawpy. Plate solving by astrometry.net. Nothing generated

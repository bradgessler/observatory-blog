**The core of the Andromeda Galaxy**: Messier 31

Night of 8 October 2026, San Francisco Bay Area

**Equipment**

* Telescope: Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain: 203 mm aperture, 2,083 mm focal length by plate solve of this stack, f/10.3
* Camera: Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus: APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
* Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, unguided. Set down without leveling, 13.4 degrees off the pole; aligned from four plate-solved photos, both axes driven from that model
* Computer: Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir

**Acquisition**

* Lights: 30 × 15 s at ISO 3200 (7.5 minutes)
* Frames: 33 taken, 30 kept
* When: 8 October 2026, late evening, over 17 minutes. 75 to 79 degrees up, no Moon
* Sky: Clear and moonless, with one patch of thin cloud that crossed the Ring Nebula for eight minutes. Steady air late in the night
* Calibration: No darks and no flat frames were taken. The flat is built from the open sky round M76, shot 25 to 55 minutes earlier with nothing moved but the mount; it agrees with the 3 October flat to 1 percent in green and blue. 50 dust shadows divided out
* Field: 38.3 x 25.6 arcmin
* Sampling: 0.776 arcsec per pixel

**Processing**

* Stacking: RAW colour planes (no demosaic), hot pixels by 3 x 3 median, registered on stars with rotation, sigma-clipped weighted mean, one constant per frame. Noise 5.6 times lower than one frame (5.5 expected)
* Finish: The camera's as-shot white balance, no colour matrix. Arcsinh stretch with the nucleus unclipped (250 of 255). Gamma 1.2, S-curve 0.45, saturation x 1.2. Faint glow made neutral, dark parts smoothed (bilateral, 2.5 px)
* Software: Capture and mount: Observatory (Elixir on a Raspberry Pi 3). Stacking: numpy, SciPy, OpenCV, rawpy. Plate solving: astrometry.net. Stacked from the camera's RAW frames. Nothing generated

**NGC 1535**: Cleopatra's Eye, a planetary nebula in Eridanus

Night of 12 October 2026, San Francisco Bay Area

**Equipment**

* Telescope: Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain: 203 mm aperture, 2,081.2 mm focal length by plate solve of this stack, f/10.3
* Camera: Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus: APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
* Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, unguided. Set down without polar alignment, about 5 degrees off the pole; aligned from four plate-solved photos (about 1 arcminute), both axes driven from that model
* Computer: Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir

**Acquisition**

* Lights: 30 × 4 s at ISO 1600 (120 seconds)
* Frames: 33 taken, 30 kept
* When: 13 October 2026, after midnight, over 17 minutes. About 40 degrees up
* Sky: Clear and calm until the last hour and a half, when thin cloud came through in patches
* Calibration: No darks and no flat frames were taken. Divided by the night's flat, built from the open sky of the NGC 891 run. This run's own sky is too faint in 4 s to check dust near the nebula; the odd and the even frames, each with frames of both runs, agree to 1 percent on the nebula. Hot pixels from the run itself (690 to 760 per colour plane)
* Field: 9.3 x 6.2 arcmin
* Sampling: 0.777 arcsec per pixel

**Processing**

* Registration: Rotation and shift on 35 to 70 stars; the field turned 0.38 degrees. The air is a prism this low: red and blue star images sat 0.79 arcsec apart, measured on 22 stars and taken out in the one resampling (0.02 left). For the nebula's own light, which is not starlight, blue is still about 0.15 arcsec off
* Stacking: RAW colour planes (no demosaic), hot pixels by 3 x 3 median, registered on stars with rotation, sigma-clipped weighted mean. One sky constant per colour per frame; in the picture only, a sky plane per colour, under 1.5 DN across it
* Finish: Brightness smoothed 1 px, colour 3.5 px, arcsinh stretch. Black at the 60th percentile, S-curve 0.15. Dark sky smoothed (median 3 px, bilateral 4 px) but kept below the shell on purpose: the shell's outer edge is grainy at full size, which is the stack's own noise
* Software: Capture and mount: Observatory (Elixir on a Raspberry Pi 3). Stacking: numpy, OpenCV, rawpy. Plate solving: astrometry.net. Stacked from the camera's RAW frames. Nothing generated

**NGC 2392**: A planetary nebula in Gemini

Night of 12 October 2026, San Francisco Bay Area

**Equipment**

* Telescope: Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain: 203 mm aperture, 2,081.7 mm focal length by plate solve of this stack, f/10.3
* Camera: Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus: APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
* Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, unguided. Set down without polar alignment, about 5 degrees off the pole; aligned from four plate-solved photos (about 1 arcminute), both axes driven from that model
* Computer: Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir

**Acquisition**

* Lights: 21 × 4 s at ISO 1600 (84 seconds)
* Frames: 32 taken, 21 kept
* When: 13 October 2026, after midnight, over 10 minutes. About 50 degrees up
* Sky: Clear and calm until the last hour and a half, when thin cloud came through in patches
* Calibration: No darks and no flat frames were taken. Divided by the night's flat, built from the open sky of the NGC 891 run. This run's own sky is too faint in 4 s to check the flat's dust, and the nebula sat on the same pixels throughout, so a shadow there would not average out. Hot pixels from the run itself (580 to 620 per colour plane)
* Field: 9.3 x 6.2 arcmin
* Sampling: 0.777 arcsec per pixel

**Processing**

* Registration: Rotation and shift on 81 to 156 stars, 0.8 to 1.1 px rms; the field turned 0.23 degrees over the frames used. The air spread red from blue by 0.48 arcsec: measured on 62 stars and taken out in the one resampling
* Stacking: RAW colour planes (no demosaic), hot pixels by 3 x 3 median, registered on stars with rotation, sigma-clipped weighted mean. One sky constant per colour per frame; in the picture only, a sky plane per colour is subtracted as well
* Finish: Brightness smoothed 1.5 px (NGC 1535: 1.0), colour 3.5 px, arcsinh stretch. Black at the 60th percentile, S-curve 0.15. Dark sky smoothed (median 3 px, bilateral 4 px). The 8th magnitude star north of the nebula is five times brighter than the nebula's centre: its core is at full scale in green for a few pixels
* Software: Capture and mount: Observatory (Elixir on a Raspberry Pi 3). Stacking: numpy, OpenCV, rawpy. Plate solving: astrometry.net. Stacked from the camera's RAW frames. Nothing generated

**The Crab Nebula**: Messier 1, the wreck of an exploded star in Taurus

Night of 12 October 2026, San Francisco Bay Area

**Equipment**

* Telescope: Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain: 203 mm aperture, 2,083 mm focal length by plate solve of this stack, f/10.3
* Camera: Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus: APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
* Mount: Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, unguided. Set down without polar alignment, about 5 degrees off the pole; aligned from four plate-solved photos (about 1 arcminute), both axes driven from that model
* Computer: Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir

**Acquisition**

* Lights: 37 × 15 s at ISO 3200 (9.25 minutes)
* Frames: 41 taken, 37 kept
* When: 13 October 2026, after midnight, over 27 minutes. About 60 degrees up
* Sky: Clear and calm until the last hour and a half, when thin cloud came through in patches
* Calibration: No darks and no flat frames were taken. The night's flat, built from the open sky of the NGC 891 run, and 133 dust shadows it left, measured on this run's own sky and divided out. The sky just round the nebula sits about 1 percent low, which trims the nebula's faintest edge a little. Hot pixels from the run itself
* Field: 10.4 x 7.8 arcmin
* Sampling: 0.776 arcsec per pixel

**Processing**

* Stacking: RAW colour planes (no demosaic), hot pixels by 3 x 3 median, registered on stars with rotation, sigma-clipped weighted mean. One sky constant per colour per frame; in the picture only, a sky plane per colour is subtracted as well. Noise 5.5 to 5.7 times lower than one frame (6.1 expected from the weights)
* Finish: Warm white, red : green : blue 1.10 : 1 : 0.83 in the camera's daylight white balance, core and rim alike. The colour comes from a 32 px average, so the stars are shown white: a side effect, not their colour. Arcsinh stretch, black at the 60th percentile, S-curve 0.15, no saturation added. The dark sky is set near black and smoothed (median 5 px, bilateral 5 px), by choice: 2.8 percent of the picture is exactly black
* Software: Capture and mount: Observatory (Elixir on a Raspberry Pi 3). Stacking: numpy, OpenCV, rawpy. Plate solving: astrometry.net. Stacked from the camera's RAW frames. Nothing generated

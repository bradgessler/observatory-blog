Night of 3 October 2026

Nine pictures from one night: the Orion Nebula, a last-quarter Moon, the Pleiades, the Andromeda Galaxy, and Saturn with seven of its moons.

  • The Orion Nebula Messier 42
  • The Moon at last quarter 4 October 2026 · 41 percent lit
  • The Pleiades Messier 45, the Seven Sisters
  • The heart of the Andromeda Galaxy Messier 31
  • Saturn and seven of its moons At opposition, 4 October 2026
  • The Orion Nebula, wide Messier 42 and Messier 43
  • The Andromeda Galaxy: core, dust lanes and a companion Messier 31 and Messier 32
  • Messier 15 A globular cluster in Pegasus
  • The Blue Snowball NGC 7662, a planetary nebula in Andromeda

The telescope ran itself for most of this. Claude drove it through the software while I turned the focus knob, and then I went to bed. By dawn there were 990 frames on the card.

Thin cloud came through all night, so a lot of frames were thrown away. Every picture's page says how many were taken and how many were kept. Two targets never happened: the Ring Nebula and the Dumbbell were behind the tree.

Pick a picture to see what it is, how far away it is, and how it was made.

Location
San Francisco Bay Area. A yard at home, with a big tree to the west
Sky
Thin cloud drifting through all night, and a 41 percent Moon from the small hours. Warm, dry air
Telescope
Celestron 8SE Schmidt-Cassegrain with a dew shield. 203 mm aperture, 2,084 mm focal length by plate solve, f/10.3
Camera
Sony a6000, stock, at prime focus. APS-C 23.5 x 15.6 mm, 6000 x 4000, 3.9 micron pixels, RAW
Field
38.8 x 25.9 arcmin in one frame, 0.388 arcsec per pixel
Mount
Sky-Watcher EQ6-R, unguided and not polar aligned. Set down 27 degrees off the pole, both axes driven from a pointing model fitted to plate solves
Computer
Raspberry Pi 3 running Observatory, in Elixir
Frames
990 RAW frames between dusk and dawn
Processing
Stacked from the RAW frames with numpy, OpenCV and rawpy. Plate solving by astrometry.net. Nothing generated
